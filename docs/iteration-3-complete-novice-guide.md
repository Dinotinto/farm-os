# Dinotinto Farm OS — Complete Novice Step-by-Step Guide

This guide explains how to use the app from scratch, assuming the user has never used farm software before.

## Big idea

Dinotinto Farm OS works best when records are entered in this order:

1. Add your land/plots.
2. Add your workers.
3. Add the crops you actually want to grow.
4. Add irrigation materials and costs.
5. Create irrigation or clearing plans for each plot.
6. Create a planting plan.
7. Add tasks and assign workers.
8. Record input costs, attendance, salaries, market prices and observations.
9. Use reports to see cost, yield and profit.

Do not worry if you do not have all the information at once. Start with land, workers and crops, then add costs as they happen.

---

## 1. Dashboard

Use the Dashboard to see the whole farm in one place.

It shows:

- total mapped acres
- irrigated acres
- plots needing work
- open tasks
- unpaid labour or salary balances
- expected crop profit
- irrigation setup costs

If you want a clean system, use **Clear all records and start fresh** on the Dashboard. This removes existing records so you can enter your own real farm data.

Use this carefully because it clears records from the working data set.

---

## 2. Farm Map / Acre Registry

This is where you add your land.

To add a plot:

1. Go to **Farm Map**.
2. Enter the plot name, for example `Shimawa Acre A`.
3. Enter the plot code, for example `Acre A`.
4. Enter length and width, for example `300` by `100`.
5. Choose feet or metres.
6. Choose whether it is irrigated.
7. Choose the land status, for example ready, bushy, needs clearing or waterlogged.
8. Add soil notes or access notes if needed.
9. Save the plot.

The app calculates the area and equivalent acres automatically.

Delete any plot you do not want by using the delete button on the plot card.

---

## 3. Workers

This is where you add farm workers, daily labourers, weekly staff, monthly staff or contractors.

To add a worker:

1. Go to **Workers**.
2. Enter the worker name.
3. Add phone number if available.
4. Add role, for example `Sprayer`, `Field lead`, `Daily labourer` or `Harvester`.
5. Choose employment type.
6. Enter salary or daily rate.
7. Add skills and notes.
8. Save.

To edit a worker:

1. Find the worker card.
2. Click **Edit worker**.
3. Change the phone, role, rate, status or notes.
4. Save worker changes.

To remove a worker, click **Delete worker**.

---

## 4. Salary and Payroll

Use this page for proper wage tracking.

Basic flow:

1. Add workers first.
2. Go to **Salary & Payroll**.
3. Record attendance or days worked.
4. Add allowances, deductions, advances or bonuses if needed.
5. Create a salary run for the worker.
6. Enter the amount paid.
7. The app calculates balance.

Use salary status to track whether pay is draft, approved, part paid, paid or cancelled.

---

## 5. Crop Catalog

This is where you enter the crops you actually grow.

To add a crop:

1. Go to **Crop Catalog**.
2. Enter crop name, for example pepper, cucumber, sweet corn, garden egg or plantain.
3. Choose planting method.
4. Choose whether nursery is required.
5. Enter nursery duration if known.
6. Select water need.
7. Choose whether irrigation is recommended.
8. Add expected harvest duration and expected yield per acre.
9. Add common fertiliser stages, pest risks and care tasks.
10. Save.

Delete crops you do not want so your planting planner only uses your real crop list.

---

## 6. Irrigation Materials

This is where you enter the real cost of irrigation materials.

You can either add a custom item or start from a prepared list.

Prepared examples include:

- submersible pump
- water tank
- main line pipe
- sub-main line pipe
- drip lines
- rain gun sprinkler
- connectors and valves
- water filter
- hose
- solar/inverter setup
- installation labour
- transport

To add a material:

1. Go to **Irrigation**.
2. Choose an item from **Start from prepared list**, or choose custom.
3. Enter the actual item name.
4. Enter quantity.
5. Enter unit, for example roll, unit, trip, tank or worker-days.
6. Enter unit cost.
7. Enter supplier if known.
8. Choose whether the item is reusable.
9. Save.

The material table calculates total cost for each row.

Delete any row that is wrong or not needed.

---

## 7. Irrigation Plan for a Plot

Use this when you want to answer a question like:

> How much will it cost to irrigate Acre C?

Steps:

1. Add the plot in Farm Map.
2. Add all relevant irrigation materials.
3. Go to **Irrigation**.
4. In the plan builder, choose the plot.
5. Choose irrigation method, for example drip, rain gun, sprinkler or manual.
6. Tick only the material rows needed for that plot.
7. Enter water source and setup time if known.
8. Leave expected cost blank if you want the app to calculate it from selected materials.
9. Save the irrigation plan.

The saved plan shows cost, maintenance estimate, materials used and setup details.

---

## 8. Land Clearing

Use this before planting if a plot is bushy, waterlogged, too dry or not ready.

Steps:

1. Go to **Land Clearing**.
2. Select the plot.
3. Select current condition.
4. Choose recommended method, for example manual labour, herbicide, mechanical clearing, slashing, ploughing or combined method.
5. Enter labour, chemical, equipment, estimated cost and time.
6. Save the plan.

Use this to compare clearing options before spending money.

---

## 9. Planting Planner

Use this when you are ready to plan a crop on a plot.

Steps:

1. Add your plot first.
2. Add your crop in Crop Catalog.
3. Go to **Planting Planner**.
4. Choose crop.
5. Choose plot.
6. Enter planned nursery, planting or transplant dates.
7. Enter seed cost, fertiliser budget, labour budget, irrigation budget and transport budget.
8. Enter expected yield and expected market price.
9. Save.

The app will warn if:

- the plot is bushy
- clearing is needed
- the crop needs water but the plot is not irrigated
- seed cost is missing
- fertiliser plan is missing
- market price is missing

---

## 10. Inputs and Costs

Use this page to record what you actually spend.

Examples:

- seeds
- fertiliser
- manure
- pesticide
- insecticide
- herbicide
- fungicide
- labour
- irrigation
- transport
- packaging
- equipment rental
- fuel

Each cost can be linked to a plot, crop cycle or planting plan.

Delete wrong entries and re-enter them correctly if needed.

---

## 11. Tasks

Use Tasks to plan farm work.

Examples:

- clearing
- nursery work
- planting
- transplanting
- watering
- fertiliser
- spraying
- weeding
- harvesting
- packing
- transport
- irrigation repair
- inspection
- market survey

Steps:

1. Go to **Tasks**.
2. Enter task title.
3. Choose task type.
4. Link to crop and plot if relevant.
5. Assign a worker.
6. Set due date and priority.
7. Save.

Update task status when work is pending, in progress, completed or cancelled.

---

## 12. Market Prices

Use this page to record current market prices.

For example:

- cucumber per 40kg bag
- tomato per crate
- pepper per basket
- sweet corn per piece or bag
- plantain per bunch

Enter the market, date checked, price source and confidence level.

These prices help yield and profit forecasts.

---

## 13. Yield Forecast

Use Yield Forecast to see expected revenue and profit.

The forecast uses:

- expected yield
- loss percentage
- marketable yield
- market price
- expected cost

If the forecast is wrong, update the related planting plan, market price or observation.

---

## 14. Observations

Use Observations during the crop cycle.

Examples:

- pest attack
- disease
- poor germination
- heavy rainfall
- dry spell
- flooding
- fertiliser delay
- labour delay
- market price change
- theft/loss
- plant stress

Enter severity, yield impact and cost impact. This helps explain why profit or yield changed.

---

## 15. Reports

Reports bring everything together.

Use Reports to check:

- land status
- irrigation coverage
- crop profit forecast
- labour cost
- salary cost
- attendance
- input cost
- market prices
- upcoming tasks
- overdue tasks
- plot readiness

Reports only become useful after your own farm data has been entered.
